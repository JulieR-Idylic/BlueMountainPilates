Where are your eyes?

One of the smallest cues in Pilates can have one of the biggest effects: your gaze. I often mention, "Where your eyes go, the body tends to follow." While that isn't true for every exercise, it is surprising how often your gaze influences your posture, balance, and the direction of your movement.

Allow your eyes to support the shape of the exercise, rather than create it.

If your eyes become fixed on a spot, your head may begin to lead the movement instead of staying connected to the spine. But when your gaze moves with intention, your body often finds better alignment, length, and flow. And occasionally, I'll even ask you to close your eyes. By removing visual input, your body has an opportunity to rely on balance, proprioception, and the sensations of the movement itself.

This week, notice where your eyes are looking during exercises like Down Stretch, Elephant, Short Box Flat Back, or Straps Pulling. And if I ask you to close your eyes during Teaser, trust that there's a reason. 😊
