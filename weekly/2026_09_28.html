Good morning Pilates Community! This week's Pilates pearl before getting into the schedule:

What does it mean to advance in Pilates? Part 2 😊

Last week, we talked about three different ways we can progress in Pilates: repertoire, movement, and practice & independence. We focused on movement and how even something as familiar as Footwork can continue to become deeper and more advanced.

This week, let's talk about practice & independence. When you're first learning Pilates, there is a LOT to remember! The name of the exercise, how to set up, where your body goes, what the springs are doing – and then somehow you're supposed to actually move. 😊

Over time, something starts to change. You may still need your instructor to tell you what's next or remind you of the spring setting – that's not the important part. What changes is that when your instructor gives you the name of a familiar exercise, you begin to know what to do with it. You can start setting yourself up. You know the shape you're trying to create. You can organize your body and begin the movement without needing your instructor to walk you through every piece of it.

That's independence – and that's progression.

It also requires your instructor to give you the opportunity to do it! I'll admit, this one can be hard for me. 😊 Sometimes I have to resist the urge to immediately start cueing your setup and give you a moment to find it yourself. That little bit of space allows you to start trusting what you already know.

And it changes what your instructor can give you. If less time is needed to explain the basic setup and movement, there's more opportunity to watch how you're moving and help you find the next layer. Can you find more opposition? More reach? More precision? Can one small cue change how the whole exercise feels?

There's another benefit – Flow, one of our Pilates principles. As an exercise becomes more familiar, you don't have to stop and think through every piece of the setup before you begin. You can organize yourself and move into the exercise with more confidence and continuity.

Independence doesn't mean remembering the entire order of your workout. It's absolutely okay not to remember what comes next – that's one of the reasons your instructor is there! Lean on us for the order, spring settings or a reminder when you need it. We'd much rather have you fully present in the exercise you're doing than thinking ahead and trying to remember what comes next. Knowing your practice is different from memorizing your workout.

As your practice develops, the goal isn't to need less teaching. It's to create room for deeper teaching – and more Flow. This week, when your instructor gives you the name of a familiar exercise, take a moment and notice how much your body already knows. 😊
