If you notice someone in class approaching an exercise a little differently this week, they may be experimenting with some of the concepts we explored during the workshop. One of the themes we discussed was that advanced Pilates isn't always about learning new exercises – it's about finding greater depth, connection, and understanding within familiar ones.

Which leads me into this week’s Pilates wisdom: What does it mean to advance in Pilates?

We often think advancing means learning harder exercises. And yes, repertoire is one way we progress in Pilates. But it’s only one.

I think about progression in three different ways:

  • Repertoire – expanding the exercises you know and are ready to perform.
  • Movement – developing greater quality, depth and understanding within the exercises you already know.
  • Practice & independence – becoming more confident in your own practice and less dependent on your instructor to guide every part of it.

This week, let’s focus on movement.

At the workshop, the very first exercise we explored was Footwork. It doesn't get much more foundational than that! But think about how much depth we can add to Footwork without changing the exercise at all.

Can you bring concentration to what your whole body is doing, rather than just moving the carriage in and out? Can you maintain your center and organize what needs to stay stable while your legs move? Can you use control throughout the entire movement, including that last inch home? Can you add precision to the placement and movement of your feet, knees and legs? Can your breath support the movement rather than stop when you're concentrating? And can all of that come together with flow, so the exercise remains continuous rather than becoming a series of separate actions?

Then add opposition. As your legs reach toward the footbar, what is reaching in the other direction? Can you stay grounded through your pelvis and spine while creating length through the legs? Can you maintain that opposition as the carriage travels out and as it comes home?

Suddenly, Footwork isn't quite so basic.

To someone watching, the movement may look very much the same – the carriage travels out and comes back in. But within that movement, you can continue to explore greater control, precision, concentration, centering, breath and flow, along with deeper opposition and connection.

That's what we mean by advancing the movement. The exercise doesn't necessarily need to look harder or more complicated. The depth of what you're doing within it has changed.

The repertoire hasn't advanced – the movement has.

This week, when you get set to start Footwork, see how many of these layers you can bring into an exercise you've probably done hundreds of times. There is always more to discover. 😊
