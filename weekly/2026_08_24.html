Good morning 😊 Here’s another gem to think about this week in your sessions:

Last week, we listened to the sounds of the apparatus and what they might tell us about our movement. This week, let's pay attention to the feedback we can feel.

Have you ever felt the Reformer carriage bounce underneath you? Or felt a little change or jerk in the tension of the straps? What is the apparatus telling you when that happens?

During Reformer exercises like Rowing, Pulling Straps/T, Teaser with Straps, Chest Expansion, Leg Circles or Frogs, the springs and straps create a continuous connection between you and the apparatus. When that connection suddenly changes—or the carriage begins to bounce—it can be a clue that momentum has entered the exercise.

You can feel similar feedback on the Cadillac. During the Push Through Series, for example, notice the flow of the spring resistance. Does it remain smooth and continuous, or can you feel a change in that flow as you move or change direction?

You may have heard me say in class that momentum is the Pilates killer! Once momentum takes over, we're no longer controlling every part of the movement—we've allowed the movement itself to do some of the work for us.

Instead, notice whether you can maintain that continuous connection with the apparatus. Can you feel the resistance throughout the entire movement, even as you change direction?

This week, pay attention when you feel a bounce, a jerk, or a change in the flow of resistance. Rather than simply continuing, consider what that feedback might be telling you.

Are you moving the apparatus, or has the apparatus started moving you?

I hope you've enjoyed the past few weeks of Pilates wisdoms and that they've helped you bring a little more awareness and curiosity into your practice. 😊
