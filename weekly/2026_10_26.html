This week's Pilates pearl:

You've probably heard me say in class that we work with the body that came in the door. Some days we feel strong and energetic, other days we're tired, stiff, or just not moving quite the way we usually do. But what happens when we've injured ourselves?

The good news is that an injury doesn't necessarily mean you have to stop doing Pilates! In many cases, we can modify exercises to work around an injury while still getting plenty of benefit from your session.

For example:

  • A sore shoulder or wrist? We may avoid exercises that put weight through the arms or require certain movements, while continuing to work on other parts of the body.
  • A strained muscle or sore knee? We can often adjust your range of motion, spring resistance, or exercise selection to avoid aggravating the injury.

But there are times when we need to be more cautious. Injuries involving possible nerve symptoms, such as radiating pain, tingling, numbness, or weakness, deserve special attention. The same goes for more serious or unexplained injuries. In these situations, it's recommended that you consult with your healthcare provider before continuing Pilates – or take some time to rest.

The important thing is to communicate with your instructor! Let us know what happened, what you're feeling, and whether you've received any guidance or restrictions from your healthcare provider. Don't wait until an exercise hurts to mention it.

And remember, working around an injury doesn't mean working through pain. Sometimes the right choice is to modify, sometimes it's to rest a particular area, and sometimes it's to take a break until you're ready to return.

Our goal is always the same: to work safely and effectively with the body that came in the door that day. 😊
