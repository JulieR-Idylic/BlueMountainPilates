Good Morning, Pilates Community! A little Pilates inspiration for your week 😊

The last two weeks we explored how our eyes support movement. This week let's move to the other end of the body – our feet.

In many Reformer exercises, your feet provide the grounding that allows the rest of your body to create opposition. Without that steady connection, it's much harder to lengthen, stabilize, and move with control.

Sometimes, as the carriage moves away, it’s natural to curl our toes around the footbar. Usually that's not because the toes need to work harder – it's because we've lost that feeling of grounding. The movement has become faster than the connection.

Instead, see if you can stay in conversation with the footbar. Rather than gripping with your toes, maintain gentle, even pressure through your whole foot as the carriage moves away and returns. That steady connection gives your body the grounding it needs to create opposition throughout the exercise.

This week, notice your feet during exercises like Footwork, Stomach Massage, Pelvic Lift or Prancing/Running. If your toes begin to grip, don't just try to relax them. Ask yourself: "Have I lost my grounding?" Then see if slowing the movement just a touch helps you stay connected to the footbar—and notice what that does for the rest of your body.

I'd love to hear what you discover.
